Pans & Pots

Vessels for stovetop cooking

Fry Pan ø 14-32 cm

For searing as it allows steam to escape for browning.

Sauté Pan ø 14-32 cm

For reducing liquids as it provides large surface area.

Sauce Pan ø 16-22 cm

Heating liquids as straight edge prevents quick evaporation.

Saucier ø 17-24 cm

For applications that needs whisking and stirring.

Stock Pot ø 28 cm

Height is same as diameter. For simmering in a lot of liquid.

Soup Pot ø 22 cm

Taller than it is wide. For soups that requires little evaporation.

Stew Pot ø 28 cm

Height is just over ½ diameter. For soups and stew.

Rondeau ø 28 cm

Height is about 1/3 diameter. For shallow frying, searing, and braising.

Cast Iron Skillet ø 15-50 cm

Usually made from cast iron. For searing.

Wok ø 20-36 cm

For active cooking such as stir fry.

09

Cooking Methods

Heat application techniques

Dry Heat

Sauté

Small amount of fat. High heat. Food often left to brown to develop a fond.

Pan Fry

Very small amount of fat. Medium heat for even cooking.

Shallow Fry

Food is partly submerged in cooking fat and turned over during cooking.

Deep Fry

Food is fully submerged in cooking fat until fully cooked.

Stir Fry

Small amount of fat. Very high heat. Food stirred and tossed quickly.

Bake/Roast

Indirect and evenly distributed heat around the food.

Broil/Grill

Direct heat from either the top (broil) or bottom (grill).

Smoke

Food is exposed to smoke from smouldering material(s), usually wood chips.
